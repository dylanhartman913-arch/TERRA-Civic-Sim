# TERRA — A County-Level Energy Transition Simulator for the Mountain West

*A brief for an advisor conversation. Written 2026-09-11.*

## What it is

TERRA (Transition & Ecological Resilience Research Architecture) is a participatory simulation tool in which a person — a county commissioner, a planner, a workshop participant, a student — sits in front of a map of 157 Mountain West counties and builds a future. They place power plants, transmission upgrades, data centers, restoration projects, housing, workforce programs, and agricultural treatments; they advance the clock year by year through 2050 and beyond; and the tool shows them, county by county, what their choices cost and yield: in electricity, in jobs, in property tax and school funding, in water, in land, in ecological condition, and in what the county can withstand when a drought or a heat wave arrives.

The design premise is that energy transition decisions are made at the county scale — counties permit, zone, tax, and elect — but the tools that model those decisions operate at the scale of the grid or the nation, in units nobody lives in. TERRA puts a model-grounded simulator in the unit where decisions actually happen, and it treats the consequences that matter locally (the Campbell County school budget, the Laramie County housing market, the forage on a Converse County allotment) as first-class outputs rather than afterthoughts.

## What goes into it

The tool sits on top of roughly nine data pipelines, each built from public sources with every coefficient traced to a citation and flagged with a confidence level.

The energy-system foundation is a 500-bus synthetic transmission network for the continental US, over-resolved in the Mountain West and calibrated so that balancing-authority transfer envelopes reproduce observed EIA-930 interchange, with a complete EIA-860 operating-generator inventory (25,868 records, hash-pinned) assigned to buses and costed from NREL ATB 2024. Four capacity-expansion scenarios (baseline, $50 carbon price, IRA incentives, both) were solved on this network with E4ST and serve as "oracles" — model-validated reference points the simulator compares itself against.

On top of that sits an Environmental–Economic–Social capital layer: census-tract indicators from ACS, BEA, NLCD, EPA water data, and EPA ecoregions, normalized to the regional distribution and aggregated to counties. A Wyoming-resolution fiscal ledger, built from Department of Revenue tables and BLS employment data, models assessed valuation, ad valorem and severance revenue, and the school-finance recapture mechanism that makes coal decline fiscally non-obvious. Existing assets — Powder River Basin mines, Jim Bridger, Dave Johnston, Kemmerer Unit 1, the Cheyenne data-center campuses — are live, decrementable state, so decline can be modeled alongside growth. A climate layer brings CMIP6-downscaled projections under two SSP lenses plus a historical baseline, coupled to seeded, replay-deterministic hazard events; the doctrine is that climate is a boundary condition the player chooses, never a variable their actions move. An agricultural layer adds land, water (diversion and consumptive use tracked separately), forage, federal grazing allotments, and invasive-cover dynamics for Wyoming's 23 counties, so that energy siting competes with ranching honestly.

The simulation engine exists twice — a Python reference and a TypeScript port — held in numeric parity by frozen golden fixtures across five independent digest contracts. Every action draws from era-level resource pools (capital, labor, steel, concrete, HALEU, transmission right-of-way) and appends to a material ledger. The app runs entirely in the browser with no backend; a session exports as a single replayable file whose digest proves it was not edited.

## Vision and purpose

The purpose is a defensible, honest instrument for participatory energy planning under uncertainty. Three commitments define it. Every number is traceable: a user can travel from a headline gauge to the derived tier to the raw ledger to the source citation in a few clicks. Uncertainty is disclosed, not smoothed: the energy-system response is model-validated, the ecological and social responses are coefficient-based estimates, and the interface says which is which. And the tool shows consequences without forbidding choices: placing a data center where there is no firm supply produces a visible deficit, not an error message.

Methodologically, the contribution is participatory simulation under exogenous climate uncertainty at the governance scale, with a reproducibility discipline (provenance manifests, golden-fixture parity, deterministic replay) that lets any participant's pathway be independently re-executed and any claim in a methods chapter be checked against the shipped code.

## What a fully developed version could be used for

For county governments and regional planners, it is a scenario-screening tool: what does a coal-plant retirement, a nuclear build, or a hyperscale data center do to this county's revenue, labor force, water, and housing over twenty years, and which combinations of actions keep the county whole. For facilitated workshops — the near-term target is a Ruckelshaus-style convening — it is a structured deliberation instrument: participants build pathways under identical seeded futures, and a facilitator dashboard surfaces where the room converges, where it forks, and what people said at the moment they chose. For state fiscal analysis, the Wyoming ledger can trace the decoupling of mineral revenue from school funding under any retirement schedule. For extension and land-use offices, the agricultural layer makes energy–ranching coexistence a computable question rather than a rhetorical one. For teaching, it is a systems-thinking environment where students discover resource constraints and coupling effects by running into them. And for research, the replayable session files are a dataset: the tool is an experimental apparatus for studying how people reason about long-horizon tradeoffs, and its plugin structure (transmission headroom, wildlife and siting constraints, and workforce–housing dynamics are the next three) lets new constraint layers be added without redesigning the schema.

## Where it stands, honestly

The engine, the parity discipline, and the newer data pipelines are further along than the validation. An independent audit this month found the program functionally complete but carrying consolidation debt: work unpushed, two data-promotion defects live, no continuous integration, and the upstream notebooks archived out of the main branch. A hardening wave is planned and scoped before any new capability is added. What the project most needs is not more code but expert judgment on validation — whether the coefficient-based layers are defensible as a dissertation method, whether the four-scenario oracle is sufficient grounding for the energy-system claims, how to design the workshop study so the participatory claim carries evidence, and which of the next plugins makes the best first paper.
